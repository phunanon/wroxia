# Instruments

Instruments are part of this constitution and protected like its articles. They
hold procedures used by the articles, so that the articles stay short. An
article uses an instrument by naming it and giving a value for each of its
parameters, which are written in _italics_.

## Instrument 1. Sticky sortition

**Parameters:** _seats_, _maximum service_, _round interval_, _cohorts_.

1. The target number of seats is _seats_, rounded down to a multiple of
   _cohorts_, with a minimum of _cohorts_. The body begins with that number of
   seats.
2. Every _round interval_, the N members who have gone longest since joining or
   last being reconfirmed face reconfirmation.
   - N is the number of seats divided by _cohorts_.
3. Each citizen may score each member facing reconfirmation from 0 to 5. A
   member averaging 2.5 or more keeps their seat.
4. A member leaves on falling below 2.5, on reaching _maximum service_ in total,
   or on resigning or dying.
5. At each round, a body below its target sets its number of seats halfway
   between its current number and the target, rounded down to a multiple of
   _cohorts_.
6. At each round, a body above its target removes only empty seats. The number
   removed is the greatest non-negative multiple of _cohorts_ not exceeding
   either half the empty seats or the difference between the current number of
   seats and the target.
7. All remaining empty seats are filled by lot from citizens who put themselves
   forward, first from those who did not just leave by reconfirmation, then from
   those who did, highest score first, with ties settled by lot. No one is
   seated beyond _maximum service_.

## Instrument 2. Directions and custody

This instrument applies only where an article names it, and gives no power to
limit a right or privilege beyond what that article permits.

**Parameters:** _default purpose_, _Advocate_.

1. Directions may name successive people or offices; naming an office includes
   its successive holders.
2. If a direction cannot lawfully or practicably be followed, a court may permit
   only the nearest lawful, practicable course, preserving every other direction
   that can be followed.
3. If no directions can be found, anyone may act only for the _default purpose_;
   where that is disputed, a court may appoint someone to do so.
4. The court hears those named and the _Advocate_, so far as they can be
   reached, and gives reasons subject to review by another court.

## Instrument 3. Establishing capacity

This instrument concerns establishing whether someone was an agent when acting.
No assessment procedure or power is yet provided.

## Instrument 4. Majority decision

1. A body decides by a majority of those voting, with more than half its seats
   taking part.

## Instrument 5. Election

This instrument concerns electing representatives. No size, voting system, term
or procedure is yet provided.

## Instrument 6. Land

Law settles what this instrument leaves open.

1. Each human citizen has one quota. One under 18 has half, staked only on land
   to which they alone have title, and managed for their sake. Neither quotas
   nor title can be sold or given away.
2. Title is gained only by taking vacant land, by pooling, or by a government
   keeping land for public use. Whoever has title may let the land on any terms,
   admit others to pool on it, and direct, in order, who takes it first after
   them. Those who pool are directed first, by joining, have title together,
   decide by agreement, or else by a majority of what they pooled, and may
   divide the land among themselves. A government registers and is directed like
   anyone, staking nothing, and keeps land only for public use, including any in
   public use when it first comes under this instrument. Nearlaw keeps all other
   vacant land until it is taken.
3. Title ends, at no cost beyond any restitution owed under III.2, on death,
   loss of citizenship, or notice, which may be for part of the land, and the
   quota staked on it is returned. The land then falls vacant; but when a pool
   member's title ends, unless their part, in proportion to what they pooled and
   with that as its stake, is taken first, the others choose a matching part of
   the land to fall vacant. Land also falls vacant on first coming under this
   instrument.
4. A share is the area of all land under this instrument divided by all quotas,
   counted each year. The stake for vacant land is its area as a fraction of a
   share, times the quotas registered for it if more than one, but no more than
   one quota or that fraction, whichever is more. Nearlaw keeps enough vacant
   plots, to a legal standard, for anyone entitled under II.7.1 without title to
   take one at once, drawn by lot, for its stake, asking nothing for what is
   fixed to it.
5. Anyone may register, alone or together, for vacant land, or for adjacent
   pieces as one, and one registrant, drawn by lot, takes it for its stake.
   Whoever declines when drawn may not register again for a year, and another is
   drawn.
6. But those directed, alone or together, or, when land first comes under this
   instrument, whoever occupies it, may take it first, for its stake or what was
   last staked on it, whichever is more, or, on a death, if no more, all their
   quota, wherever staked, if there is one of them for each whole quota last
   staked. They give up other title as needed. A direction counts once a year
   old, or at once if its giver dies; a pool member directs only with the pool's
   consent.
7. Whoever has title may ask a sum of money for what is fixed to the land, paid
   by the next taker to them, or after their death as they directed, in money or
   as they accept, and grant other land a right of way, light, support, water,
   drainage, or services over it, short of occupying it, which binds later
   takers of either until a court finds it no longer serves, as do such rights
   in use when land first comes under this instrument. Both are shown to
   registrants. If the taker offers less, five citizens drawn by lot from all
   Wroxia each write, without seeing either sum, what a sensible taker would pay
   if no one else wanted the land, and whichever sum is nearer the middle one
   stands, or the lower if tied. The taker may pay in yearly parts; if one goes
   unpaid, their title ends as on notice, and the rest is paid first out of what
   the next taker pays for what is fixed.
8. Taking from land, for use off it, anything that no one grew, raised, or made
   there requires a licence from nearlaw. Licence rent is paid equally to
   everyone with a quota.
