# Regression tests

Cases whose outcome must hold, and Residents and Adversaries whose lives must go
a certain way. Hear the Tests that rely on any clause a change touches, and
every Test before committing. Article references use the current numbering.

### T1. Encryption ban

**Scenario:** Nearlaw bans end-to-end encryption to help catch online groomers.

**Must hold:** the ban is void.

**Relies on:** Articles III.1.1 and III.1.5, since encrypting breaches no right
or privilege.

### T2. Speed limit

**Scenario:** Nearlaw sets a 20 mph limit on residential streets. A driver
exceeds it on an empty street.

**Must hold:** the limit is valid.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.2 and III.1.1.

### T3. Off-duty diplomat

**Scenario:** A farlaw diplomat assaults a neighbour at home in Wroxia.

**Must hold:** nearlaw deals with it, and farlaw has no say. The reverse also
holds: a nearlaw official abroad on farlaw business falls under farlaw.

**Relies on:** Articles IV.2.1 and IV.2.4.

### T4. Undertaker

**Scenario:** An undertaker collects a body and carries it to the person the
deceased named.

**Must hold:** this is lawful.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4.

### T5. Seized car

**Scenario:** Police detain a suspect for two days and hold their car for a
month as evidence. The suspect is cleared.

**Must hold:** both the detention and the holding of the car stand as breaches
by nearlaw, which owes restitution for every loss.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.3, II.2.6, and III.2.1.

### T6. Password demand

**Scenario:** Police demand a suspect's phone password.

**Must hold:** the suspect may refuse, and refusing costs them nothing.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.2.

### T7. Coroner

**Scenario:** Someone dies in suspicious circumstances. The coroner wants to
examine the body to find out whether they were killed. The person the deceased
named refuses.

**Must hold:** \[Open on these facts\] whether examination follows the
deceased's directions. A custodian's refusal cannot defeat an examination
expressly directed by the deceased; neither suspicion nor the refusal alone
makes a direction unworkable under Instrument 2. T47–T51 supply the missing
boundary facts.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4.

### T8. Messages routed abroad

**Scenario:** One Wroxian keeps sending messages to another after being asked to
stop. The messages pass through servers in another country.

**Must hold:** the harassment is nearlaw's. Routing through another country puts
only the transmission, while it crosses the border, in farlaw's domain, not what
is communicated.

**Relies on:** Articles III.1.3 and IV.2.1.

### T9. Armed robbery

**Scenario:** Marcus points a handgun at a shop worker, Priya, and takes £400
from the till.

**Must hold:** pointing the gun puts Priya at unreasonable risk under II.2.2;
the taking without consent breaches II.2.6. A breach of II.2.1 through suffering
additionally requires actual fear or distress, not merely a risk of it. The
scenario states no handover or consent to invalidate. Restitution includes
returning the money.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.1, II.2.2, II.2.6 and III.2.1.

### T10. Imported gum

**Scenario:** Daniel sells imported chewing gum at his market stall.

**Must hold:** no law may forbid the sale. The stall sale is nearlaw's; the
gum's foreign origin alone does not make an outside party a party to this sale.
T30 separately tests the crossing.

**Relies on:** Articles III.1.1 and IV.2.1.

### T11. Mocking a head of state

**Scenario:** Somchai posts mockery of a public figure, who says it caused them
real distress.

**Must hold:** the post breaches nothing and cannot be forbidden. Distress at
expression is not a breach.

**Relies on:** Articles III.1.1 and III.1.3.

### T12. Consenting adults

**Scenario:** An unmarried couple spend a weekend together.

**Must hold:** this breaches nothing and cannot be forbidden.

**Relies on:** Article III.1.1.

### T13. Cattle moved

**Scenario:** Ravi loads six healthy cattle into a roomy truck with water and
drives them to market.

**Must hold:** no injury is needed: confinement in the truck engages II.2.3.
Transport for sale, rather than for the cattle's own sake, engages II.2.8 where
they cannot consent. Holding them as things is separately barred by II.1.5;
transport alone must not be treated as proof of that relationship. Where Ravi is
an agent and a breach is established, the Negative Privileges Advocate acts for
the cattle and receives any restitution for their own sake.

**Relies on:** I.2.1–2, and Articles II.1.5, II.2.3, II.2.8, III.2.2 and IV.4.3.

### T14. Bribe

**Scenario:** Sofia gives Greg, a nearlaw building inspector, £5,000 to approve
a building that has not passed its safety checks, and he approves it. Nearlaw
also pays Greg his salary.

**Must hold:** Sofia's offer breaches nothing. Greg loses his role and the
approval is void. His salary is untouched.

**Relies on:** Article III.2.6.

### T15. Landlord

**Scenario:** Alex has title to a flat. For two years he has lived with his
partner across town and let the whole flat to Sam for monthly rent. Sam stops
paying.

**Must hold:** no breach by Sam: unpaid rent is not harm. Alex's title stands
however long he is away, so Alex may ask Sam to leave, and a court may order Sam
moved.

**Relies on:** II.2.3, III.2.1, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T16. Scam

**Scenario:** A caller pretending to be Jo's bank persuades Jo to transfer her
savings.

**Must hold:** Jo's consent is void, being obtained by deception, so the taking
breaches II.2.6 and restitution is owed.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.6 and III.2.1, the term Duress and I.2.5.

### T17. Incitement

**Scenario:** A speaker urges a crowd to "teach a lesson" to a named neighbour,
and some of the crowd attack him.

**Must hold:** the attackers breach II.2.1. The speaker breaches nothing, unless
the words were themselves a threat to the neighbour.

**Relies on:** I.1.15 and Articles II.2.1 and III.1.3.

### T18. Filming police

**Scenario:** Dmitri films officers detaining someone in a public square. When
an officer tells him to step back, he moves closer to keep filming.

**Must hold:** the filming breaches nothing and cannot be forbidden. An order to
step back has force only where someone would otherwise be at real risk.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.2, III.1.1 and III.1.3.

### T19. Coercive control

**Scenario:** Marcus takes his partner Elena's phone and bank card each morning
and gives her a small daily allowance. He reads her messages every night and
tells her she may not see her sister.

**Must hold:** a breach, with restitution owed. Forbidding her to see her sister
is part of the breach, not protected expression.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.6, II.2.7 and III.1.3, the term Duress and I.2.5.

### T20. Looking in a window

**Scenario:** Anders climbs a fence into a neighbour's garden at night and looks
through a window at the people inside.

**Must hold:** a breach, both for entering land the neighbour has title to and
for watching the people inside.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.5 and II.2.7.

### T21. Reading a coworker's email

**Scenario:** Lena guesses a coworker's password, reads months of their email
and saves some of it.

**Must hold:** a breach: the messages weren't addressed or entrusted to her.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.7.

### T22. Sharing intimate photos

**Scenario:** After a breakup, Rohan sends intimate photos his former partner
gave him to a group chat of friends.

**Must hold:** keeping the photos breaches nothing, since they were entrusted to
him, but sharing them onward does. Article III.1.3 doesn't shield the sharing.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.7 and III.1.3.

### T23. Moving into an empty building

**Scenario:** Paolo and his friends move into a vacant building that nearlaw
keeps, forcing a side door to get in. A company that fitted the door years ago
says the door is still its own.

**Must hold:** no breach. The door is part of the building, which nearlaw keeps
but no one has title to, so neither the building nor the door was anyone's to
protect. The group now occupies the building, but has no claim to it without
title.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.5 and II.2.6, the term Title, I.2.3, I.2.4, V.3 and
V.4.

### T24. Leaked medical records

**Scenario:** A clerk working for nearlaw leaks a patient's medical records to a
journalist.

**Must hold:** a breach by nearlaw, which answers for its clerk. Records about a
person stay protected even when a government holds them.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.7 and III.2.4.

### T25. Told to stop at home

**Scenario:** Sam has hit his partner Ana several times in the flat they share.
Ana tells him to stop and to keep away from her.

**Must hold:** Ana's request breaches nothing; it is not itself an official
order or confinement. Where Sam is an agent and his hits caused injury or
suffering, he owes restitution. The scenario does not establish an official
removal or confinement; T31 and T32 test those separately.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.1, III.1.3 and III.2.1.

### T26. Restitution paid first

**Scenario:** Kofi's bicycle is wrecked by Lee, who has no money.

**Must hold:** Kofi is paid at once from public funds. The paying government
pursues recovery from Lee; his lack of money does not postpone payment to Kofi
or permit compelled work.

**Relies on:** Article III.2.3.

### T27. Challenging which government

**Scenario:** Farlaw customs officers inspect a parcel a Wroxian posted to a
neighbour in the next street. The sender challenges this as nearlaw's matter.

**Must hold:** the matter is nearlaw's, since nothing crossed the border and no
outside party is involved. The boundary court decides only that, not whether the
inspection was otherwise justified.

**Relies on:** Articles IV.2.1 and IV.2.5.

### T28. Bicycle taken during an errand

**Scenario:** Lars rides off on Astrid's unlocked bicycle while she runs a short
errand, and sells it to a stranger.

**Must hold:** a breach. Astrid still holds the bicycle, since holding ends only
when a thing is given away, discarded, or made part of land, and restitution is
owed.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.6, the term Holding and I.2.4.

### T29. Downloading a pirated book

**Scenario:** Aiko knowingly downloads a recent manga volume that was uploaded
without the publisher's permission, to read herself.

**Must hold:** no breach, and no law may forbid it. Copying takes nothing anyone
holds.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.6 and III.1.1.

### T30. Gum crossing the border

**Scenario:** Daniel carries a box of chewing gum from another country across
Wroxia's border.

**Must hold:** the crossing is farlaw's matter while it happens. This does not
give farlaw authority over a later domestic stall sale (T10), or decide whether
any particular customs charge is lawful.

**Relies on:** Articles IV.2.1 and IV.2.5.

### T31. Removal after harm at home

**Scenario:** Sam and Ana have title together to the flat they live in. Sam has
repeatedly hit and injured Ana. After hearing both of them, a nearlaw panel
directs officers to make Sam leave the flat. He refuses, and the officers move
him out. No attack is happening or about to happen.

**Must hold:** the removal breaches II.2.3. Neither past harm nor the panel's
decision establishes the imminent threat required by the defence condition.
Nearlaw answers for the removal. This expectation must be revisited expressly if
a later-prevention condition is adopted (P11/P48).

**Relies on:** Article II.2.3 and the shared defence condition, and Articles
III.2.4–III.2.5.

### T32. Confinement after harm at home

**Scenario:** Sam and Ana have title together to the flat they live in. Sam has
repeatedly hit and injured Ana. After hearing both of them, a nearlaw panel
directs officers to lock Sam in a room in the flat overnight. He refuses, and
the officers lock him in. No attack is happening or about to happen.

**Must hold:** the confinement breaches II.2.3. Neither past harm nor the
panel's decision establishes the imminent threat required by the defence
condition. Nearlaw answers for the confinement. This expectation must be
revisited expressly if a later-prevention condition is adopted (P11/P48).

**Relies on:** Article II.2.3 and the shared defence condition, and Articles
III.2.4–III.2.5.

### T33. Restraint during an attack

**Scenario:** Ilya raises a hammer to strike Noor and steps towards her. Jo, who
can understand others' rights and act on that understanding, holds Ilya's arms
until Noor is safely beyond his reach. Holding his arms is the only way
available to prevent the blow, causes him no injury and ends as soon as the
danger ends. Ilya complains of the restraint.

**Must hold:** the brief restraint is permitted by the defence condition on
II.2.3. Permission depends on the imminent threat, necessity, duration and
proportionality, not a power to punish Ilya.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.3 and the shared defence condition.

### T34. Restraint after danger ends

**Scenario:** Ilya raises a hammer to strike Noor and steps towards her. Jo, who
can understand others' rights and act on that understanding, holds Ilya's arms
until Noor is safely beyond his reach. Holding his arms is the only way
available to prevent the blow and causes him no injury. After the danger has
ended, with no further attack imminent, Jo locks Ilya in a room overnight
without his consent.

**Must hold:** the initial restraint is permitted, but the overnight confinement
breaches II.2.3. A justified intervention does not grant continuing custody.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.3 and the shared defence condition, and Article
III.2.1.

### T35. A wallet taken during defence

**Scenario:** Ilya raises a hammer to strike Noor and steps towards her. Jo, who
can understand others' rights and act on that understanding, holds Ilya's arms
until Noor is safely beyond his reach. Holding his arms is the only way
available to prevent the blow, causes him no injury and ends as soon as the
danger ends. While restraining him, Jo takes Ilya's wallet from his pocket
without consent and keeps it to spend its cash; taking it does nothing to
prevent the attack.

**Must hold:** the restraint is permitted, but taking the wallet breaches
II.2.6. Necessity must justify each limit separately; a justified restraint does
not authorise robbery.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.3 and II.2.6 and the shared defence condition, and
Article III.2.1.

### T36. Unnecessary danger to a bystander

**Scenario:** Ilya raises a hammer to strike Noor. Jo, who can understand
others' rights and act on that understanding, can safely hold Ilya's arms to
prevent the blow. Instead Jo fires a gun past a nearby child's head to frighten
Ilya, putting the child at an unreasonable risk of being shot.

**Must hold:** defending Noor does not justify the unnecessary risk to the child
under II.2.2. The defence condition requires necessity as well as a protective
aim.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.2 and the shared defence condition.

### T37. Entry and disarming

**Scenario:** Noor is about to be struck with a hammer inside Ilya's home, to
which Ilya has title. Jo, who can understand others' rights and act on that
understanding, enters without Ilya's consent, takes the hammer from his hand and
carries it beyond his reach. No less intrusive way to prevent the blow is
available. Jo causes no injury, leaves at once and returns the hammer when the
danger has ended and no further attack is imminent.

**Must hold:** entry and temporary interference with the hammer are permitted
only as necessary to stop the attack. The defence condition on II.2.5 and II.2.6
supplies no lasting claim to the hammer or the home.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.5 and II.2.6 and the shared defence condition.

### T38. Defence against someone unable to understand

**Scenario:** Ilya raises a hammer to strike Noor and steps towards her. Ilya
cannot understand others' rights or act on that understanding, and cannot
consent to restraint. Jo, who can understand others' rights and act on that
understanding, holds Ilya's arms until Noor is safely beyond his reach. Holding
his arms is the only way available to prevent the blow, causes him no injury and
ends as soon as the danger ends. The restraint is solely to protect Noor.

**Must hold:** necessary restraint is permitted by the shared condition on
II.2.3 and II.2.8 despite not being for Ilya's own sake. This does not make Ilya
an agent or impose restitution liability on him.

**Relies on:** I.1.3 and I.2.2, Articles II.2.3 and II.2.8 and the shared
defence condition, and Article III.2.1.

### T39. Government deciding its own case

**Scenario:** Noor claims that nearlaw officers confined her without
justification. The same officers decide her disputed claim themselves, refuse to
let her answer their account or see their evidence, give no reasons, and tell
her that their decision cannot be challenged.

**Must hold:** this is not an independent court hearing under III.2.9. Noor must
have a chance to answer the claim and evidence, reasons for a court's decision,
and a review of the claim by another court on request. Nearlaw must justify its
officers' limit under III.2.5; their assertion alone does not establish a
condition.

**Relies on:** I.1.14 and Articles III.2.4–III.2.5 and III.2.9.

### T40. Prompt review of private confinement

**Scenario:** Jo locks Ilya in a room without his consent, saying this is to
protect Noor. Ilya asks a court to review the confinement. No attack is
happening or about to happen. Jo offers only evidence that Ilya damaged a fence
a week earlier.

**Must hold:** Ilya may require prompt court review even though Jo acts
privately. Jo must justify the whole period of confinement under III.2.10; the
past damage alone does not establish the defence condition. The right to review
is not itself permission to confine.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.3 and the shared defence condition, and Article
III.2.10.

### T41. Distress at protected expression

**Scenario:** Somchai publicly mocks an official. The official feels severe
distress at the mockery and orders nearlaw officers to confine Somchai solely to
stop him speaking. His words contain no threat, demand backed by duress,
unwanted repeated communication or private information.

**Must hold:** the expression remains protected by III.1.3. Distress at that
expression cannot justify confinement through the defence condition; nearlaw
answers for the limit.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.3 and III.1.3, the shared defence condition, and
Articles III.2.4–III.2.5.

### T42. Disproportionate defence

**Scenario:** Ilya sounds an air horn beside Noor, causing her brief, mild
distress without injury or risk of injury. Jo, who can understand others' rights
and act on that understanding, cannot stop the sound immediately without
injuring Ilya. Solely to end Noor's mild distress, Jo breaks Ilya's arm.

**Must hold:** even if injury is the only available way to stop the sound
immediately, this injury is out of proportion to the suffering prevented and
breaches II.2.1. Necessity alone is not enough.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.1 and the shared defence condition.

### T43. Private drug use

**Scenario:** Aiko takes a drug alone at home. She knows its effects and freely
chooses to take it. It causes no harm or unreasonable risk to anyone else, and
she neither drives nor undertakes any task affecting others.

**Must hold:** use alone breaches nothing and cannot be forbidden merely because
it involves a drug. Freely chosen self-use does not establish a breach by
another.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.1–II.2.2 and III.1.1.

### T44. An informed drug sale

**Scenario:** Daniel sells Aiko a sealed packet of a drug. Both understand its
contents and effects and freely agree to the exchange. Its contents match his
description. The exchange causes no harm or unreasonable risk to anyone.

**Must hold:** the sale as described breaches nothing and cannot be forbidden
merely because the thing sold is a drug. This does not decide enforcement of
future promises.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.2, II.2.6 and III.1.1.

### T45. Giving a drug to someone unable to consent

**Scenario:** Daniel understands others' rights and can act on that
understanding. He gives a child a drug solely to entertain his friends. The
child cannot understand its effects or consent to taking it. The drug causes the
child pain and distress.

**Must hold:** administration breaches II.2.1 and II.2.8; neither the child's
apparent agreement nor the friends' enjoyment justifies it. The incapacity is a
stated fact, not a rule that every child lacks capacity.

**Relies on:** Articles II.2.1, II.2.8 and III.2.1.

### T46. A deceptive harmful drug sale

**Scenario:** Daniel understands others' rights and can act on that
understanding. He sells Aiko powder which he describes as a mild stimulant,
knowing it contains a different substance. Believing his description, she gives
him cash she keeps for her own use and takes the powder. It causes her severe
pain; she would not have agreed to the exchange or taking it had she known its
contents.

**Must hold:** deception invalidates the consent obtained. The taking of cash
and causing pain engage II.2.6 and II.2.1, with restitution owed. This is not a
basis for forbidding the harmless informed sale in T44.

**Relies on:** I.2.5, and Articles II.2.1, II.2.6 and III.2.1.

### T47. Successive body custodians

**Scenario:** Elin freely records that after her death a coroner is to examine
her body, then Maja is to arrange its burial. After Elin dies, Maja objects to
the examination. The coroner examines the body as directed and then hands it to
Maja.

**Must hold:** the examination and subsequent handover follow Elin's directions.
Maja's later role gives her no veto over the earlier examination.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T48. A nominee who has died

**Scenario:** Elin freely records that Maja is to arrange her burial, that Noor
is to act if Maja cannot, and that her body is not to be examined. Maja dies
before Elin. After Elin dies, Noor arranges burial without an examination.

**Must hold:** the express substitute may act; Maja's death does not cancel the
separate refusal of examination or transfer authority to Maja's heirs.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T49. An impossible burial direction

**Scenario:** Elin freely records that her body is to be buried in a particular
cemetery, never cremated or examined. Before she dies, that cemetery is
permanently destroyed. After hearing her named custodian and the Negative Rights
Advocate, a court permits burial in the nearest available cemetery, preserving
the refusals of cremation and examination and giving reasons open to review.

**Must hold:** the court may replace the impossible location with the nearest
lawful, practicable course without cancelling the other directions. The
impossibility is not a general permission to use the body.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T50. No post-death directions found

**Scenario:** After Elin dies, no directions can be found despite a search.
After hearing the Negative Rights Advocate, a court appoints Noor for safe care
and burial only, giving reasons open to review. A coroner asks Noor to authorise
an investigative examination because the death appears suspicious.

**Must hold:** the appointment permits safe care and disposal, not investigative
examination. Suspicion alone does not enlarge Noor's authority.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T51. A refusal is not an impossibility

**Scenario:** Elin freely records that her body must not be examined. After her
suspicious death, a coroner asks a court to disregard that direction solely
because an examination would help establish the cause. The body can safely be
cared for and buried without examination.

**Must hold:** investigative usefulness does not make the refusal unworkable.
The court cannot authorise the examination on that ground.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T52. Disputed restitution collection

**Scenario:** Nearlaw has paid Noor restitution from its public funds. Its
recovery service demands that Jo reimburse it. Jo disputes both responsibility
for the harm and the amount; the service refuses a court hearing and declares
its own demand final.

**Must hold:** Nearlaw, as the government that paid, recovers the sum, not an
Advocate. Its recovery service cannot finally decide disputed liability or
amounts itself; these require an independent court under III.2.9. Payment to
Noor does not itself prove Jo owes the sum demanded.

**Relies on:** I.1.14 and Articles III.2.3 and III.2.9.

### T53. Forced work to repay restitution

**Scenario:** Nearlaw has paid Kofi restitution for damage caused by Lee. Lee
has no money or held things to surrender. The recovery service orders Lee to
work for it without consent until the restitution has been repaid.

**Must hold:** recovery under III.2.3 does not override the absolute right
against compelled work. The order cannot lawfully compel Lee to work; Kofi's
payment remains due regardless of successful recovery.

**Relies on:** Articles II.1.3, III.1.5 and III.2.3.

### T54. Nearlaw law-making by lot

**Scenario:** Nearlaw's law-making body has been selected in accordance with
Instrument 1, using a maximum of 25 years' service, annual rounds and one third
facing reconfirmation. More than half its seats take part in a vote and a
majority of those voting approve a proposed law. An official refuses to
recognise the body solely because its members were not elected.

**Must hold:** selection by Instrument 1 is the prescribed route for Nearlaw's
law-makers; absence of election does not disqualify the body. The stated vote
meets Instrument 4, but the content of any law remains subject to constitutional
limits.

**Relies on:** Articles III.1.5 and IV.3.1, and Instruments 1 and 4.

### T55. Farlaw veto

**Scenario:** Farlaw's elected representatives approve a proposed law. Before it
takes effect, its separate veto body, selected in accordance with Instrument 1
using 25 years' maximum service, annual rounds and one third facing
reconfirmation, votes to veto it. More than half the veto body's seats take part
and a majority of those voting support the veto.

**Must hold:** the proposed law is vetoed under IV.3.3. The veto body does not
thereby gain power to make its own replacement law, or acquire Nearlaw's
jurisdiction.

**Relies on:** Articles IV.2.1 and IV.3.2–IV.3.3, and Instruments 1 and 4.

### T56. Human agency presumed

**Scenario:** Marcus is human. Without Priya's consent, he deliberately breaks
her arm. Priya is sentient and has threatened no one; no one faces any danger
requiring defence. Marcus disputes liability solely because Priya has supplied
no separate evidence of his ability to understand others' rights and act on that
understanding. No evidence of incapacity is offered.

**Must hold:** human agency at the time of the act is presumed under I.2.2. The
absence of separate proof of Marcus's capacity does not alone prevent a finding
of breach of II.2.1, with restitution owed. Instrument 3's unfinished procedure
does not suspend that presumption.

**Relies on:** I.1.3, I.2.2, and Articles II.2.1 and III.2.1.

### T57. Human agency presumption rebutted

**Scenario:** Marcus is human. Without Priya's consent, he deliberately breaks
her arm. Priya is sentient and has threatened no one; no one faces any danger
requiring defence. The evidence establishes that when he acted Marcus could not
understand others' rights or privileges or act on that understanding.

**Must hold:** the stated incapacity rebuts the human-agency presumption. Marcus
was not an agent when acting and therefore did not breach under I.2.2; III.2.1
does not impose personal restitution liability on him. This does not remove
Priya's protection or grant anyone power to confine Marcus afterwards.

**Relies on:** I.1.3, I.2.2, and Articles II.2.1, II.2.3 and III.2.1.

### T58. A finger pressed to a phone

**Scenario:** Police arrest Mira and, against her protests, press her finger to
her phone's fingerprint reader to unlock it and read her messages.

**Must hold:** Mira was compelled to give access to information she withholds: a
breach of II.1.2 by nearlaw, which no condition or law can excuse. Reading the
messages also breaches II.2.7.

**Relies on:** Articles II.1.2, II.2.7 and III.2.4.

### T59. A fingerprint left behind

**Scenario:** Mira drinks from a glass in a café and leaves. Police lift her
fingerprint from the glass and match it to one found at a burglary.

**Must hold:** nothing was compelled from Mira and a print left in public is not
withheld, so II.1.2 is not breached. \[Open\] whether the lifted print is a
record about her under II.2.7.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.2.

### T60. A visitor is still a patient

**Scenario:** Tomas, a tourist from abroad and not a citizen, is locked in a
hotel room overnight by its Wroxian manager, who says Wroxia's protections are
"for Wroxians". A nearlaw law also says non-citizens may be confined on request
of any citizen.

**Must hold:** Tomas is a patient, so II.2.3 protects him; the manager breaches
it and the law has no force (III.1.1, III.1.5). Origin, residence and
citizenship are irrelevant to Part II's universal articles.

**Relies on:** I.1.2, Articles II.2.3, III.1.1 and III.1.5.

### T61. National is only for citizens

**Scenario:** Tomas has lived in Wroxia for years but is not a citizen.
Nearlaw's public broadcaster offers every citizen a slot under its
equitable-access scheme. Tomas asks for one and is refused because he is not a
citizen.

**Must hold:** no breach. II.8.1 is held only by citizens, and refusing Tomas
limits no universal right or privilege.

**Relies on:** I.1.4 and Article II.8.1.

### T62. A misdelivered letter

**Scenario:** A letter addressed to Ana is delivered to Ben's flat by mistake.
Ben sees that it is not for him, opens it and reads it.

**Must hold:** Ben breaches II.2.7. Only whoever a communication is addressed or
entrusted to may read it, and mistaken delivery addresses nothing to Ben.

**Relies on:** Article II.2.7.

### T63. A threat to someone's face

**Scenario:** Standing outside Tomas's house with a crowd behind her, Dana
shouts to Tomas, "You'll be taught a lesson tonight." Nobody touches him.

**Must hold:** Dana breaches II.2.1. Her words are a threat, so III.1.3 does not
shield them, and the fear they are meant to cause is suffering. No imminent
attack is needed.

**Relies on:** I.1.11, I.1.15, and Articles II.2.1 and III.1.3.

### T64. Starving, with only a companion to eat

**Scenario:** Lost for weeks in the mountains, with no food left and no other
way to get any, Hana would otherwise die. She kills her companion Ivo and eats
him.

**Must hold:** Hana breaches II.2.1: the starvation condition excludes humans.
Had she killed and eaten a rabbit, or eaten a deer found dead, she would breach
nothing.

**Relies on:** Articles II.1.4 and II.2.1.

### T65. Eating an animal found dead

**Scenario:** Lars, who has plenty of food at home, finds a deer that died of
natural causes by the road, takes it home, and eats it.

**Must hold:** Lars breaches II.1.4. The deer left no directions, and eating is
neither safe care nor disposal; only an agent who would otherwise starve may eat
it.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.

### T66. Burying a found bird

**Scenario:** Mia finds a dead blackbird in her garden and buries it.

**Must hold:** no breach. With no directions, anyone may act for safe care and
disposal; no court is needed unless that is disputed.

**Relies on:** Article II.1.4 and Instrument 2.3.

### T67. A wanted plot

**Scenario:** A share is a hectare this year. A 500 m² plot by the harbour falls
vacant, and twelve citizens, each with a whole quota unspent, register for it.

**Must hold:** its stake is a twentieth of a quota times twelve, so three fifths
of a quota. One of the twelve is drawn by lot and takes it for that stake, or
declines, and another is drawn. Money plays no part.

**Relies on:** V.1 and V.3.

### T68. A field too big for one quota

**Scenario:** A share is a hectare. A field of 1.2 hectares falls vacant. Gus
registers alone, and Jan and Petra register together, pooling their quotas.

**Must hold:** its stake is 1.2 quotas, however many register, so Gus cannot
take it alone. If Jan and Petra take it, they may later divide it between them,
and either may give notice on part of it and get back what they staked on that
part.

**Relies on:** V.1, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T69. Early and late takers

**Scenario:** When Ada's valley came under Part V, a share was a hectare, and
Ada took a hectare for one quota. Thirty years later the number of quotas has
doubled, so a share is half a hectare. Ben arrives and wants a hectare like
Ada's.

**Must hold:** Ada keeps her hectare and pays nothing more. A hectare that falls
vacant now costs two quotas, so Ben can take it only by pooling with someone
else. Until he takes land, he can take a vacant plot at once.

**Relies on:** V.1, V.3, and V.5.

### T70. A late direction

**Scenario:** Esme took a cottage for three fifths of a quota, and directs that
her neighbour Bram take it first after her. Bram has paid £400k to a bank, to be
released to Esme once he has title. Two months later, Esme dies.

**Must hold:** Bram may take the cottage first, for three fifths of a quota, or
its stake if more, since a direction counts at once on a death. Had Esme given
notice two months after directing, it would not count, and the cottage would go
by lot. The payment breaches nothing, and the text makes no one honour it.

**Relies on:** V.2, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T71. Paying for a house

**Scenario:** Amira asked £320k for her house, then gave notice. Carla is drawn
to take the plot, and offers £290k. Five citizens drawn by lot, seeing neither
figure, write what a sensible taker would pay for the house if no one else
wanted the plot: £270k, £295k, £300k, £310k, and £340k. With its sea view, the
house would fetch £900k on an open market.

**Must hold:** the middle figure is £300k, nearer £290k than £320k, so Carla
pays £290k; the sea view counts for nothing. She pays in money, or in kind if
Amira accepts, and may pay in yearly parts. If a part goes unpaid, Carla's title
ends, and Amira is paid the rest first out of what whoever takes the plot next
pays for the house.

**Relies on:** V.3 and V.6.

### T72. A drained field

**Scenario:** Gwen drained a wet field and planted an orchard on it. She gives
notice, asking £32k for what is fixed to it. Hal is drawn and offers £22k. Five
citizens drawn by lot write £24k, £28k, £30k, £33k, and £40k.

**Must hold:** the drains and trees are part of the land and pass with it. The
middle figure is £30k, nearer £32k, so Hal pays £32k or declines.

**Relies on:** I.2.3 and V.6.

### T73. Lathes in a rented works

**Scenario:** A firm rents a works from Ivo and bolts its lathes to the floor.
They can be unbolted and moved without damage to them or the works. When the
letting ends, Ivo says the lathes are now part of his works.

**Must hold:** the lathes are not part of the land, since they can be removed
intact without harming either. The firm still holds them, and may take them away
or sell them.

**Relies on:** I.2.3, I.2.4, and II.2.6.

### T74. A sale by joining

**Scenario:** Ada has title alone to a harbour plot, for which she paid one
quota. Ben pays her £200k to let him pool a quota on it. A week later, Ada gives
notice.

**Must hold:** Ada has her quota back. Ben was directed by joining only a week
ago, so he cannot take her part first. He chooses a matching half of the plot to
fall vacant, which goes by lot like any vacancy. The payment breaches nothing.

**Relies on:** V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T75. A pool with nothing spare

**Scenario:** Ten years ago, twelve walkers each pooled a whole quota to take a
moor with a stake of twelve. Ifan gives notice. None of the others has quota
unspent.

**Must hold:** Ifan has his quota back at once. No one can pay for his part, so
a majority of what the others pooled chooses a twelfth of the moor to fall
vacant, which goes by lot. Had Ifan, with the pool's consent, directed Mair to
take his part first more than a year before, she could take it for one quota.

**Relies on:** V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T76. A widower

**Scenario:** Leo and Nina each pooled a whole quota to take a house with a
stake of two quotas. Nina dies.

**Must hold:** Leo, directed by joining, may take Nina's part at once, paying no
more than his whole quota, which is already pooled there, so he keeps the house.

**Relies on:** V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T77. An orphan

**Scenario:** Maya, 13, lives with her mother, who took their house alone for
one quota and directed that Maya take it first after her, then Maya's aunt. Her
mother dies.

**Must hold:** Maya may take the house first, paying her whole half quota,
managed for her sake. If she declines, her aunt may.

**Relies on:** V.2, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T78. Refilling the floor

**Scenario:** Nearlaw keeps fewer vacant plots than anyone landless would need.
A harbour house falls vacant with £300k asked for it, and twelve citizens and
nearlaw register. Nearlaw is drawn. Later, Nia, who has no land, asks for a plot
at once.

**Must hold:** nearlaw stakes nothing, so adds nothing to the land's stake, and
pays £300k or offers less. Nia is given a kept plot by lot, for its stake, and
pays nothing for any house on it. When she leaves, she may ask a sum for it like
anyone.

**Relies on:** II.7.1 and V.1, V.3, V.4, and V.6.

### T79. Paid to decline

**Scenario:** A fund pays each citizen drawn for a harbour plot £20k to decline,
until its employee is drawn.

**Must hold:** each who declines may not register for vacant land again for a
year. The payments breach nothing.

**Relies on:** V.3.

### T80. A drain across a neighbour

**Scenario:** Hal granted Gwen's field a right to drain across his. Hal gives
notice, and Iris, drawn for his field, wants to fill in the ditch.

**Must hold:** Iris was shown the right when she registered. It binds her until
a court finds it no longer serves Gwen's field.

**Relies on:** V.6.

### T81. A path by direction

**Scenario:** Tess directs that nearlaw take a strip along the edge of her field
first, for a public footpath. A year later she gives notice on the strip.

**Must hold:** nearlaw may take the strip first, staking nothing, and keep it
only for public use. Tess gets back the quota staked on the strip. Had she given
notice at once, the strip would have gone by lot.

**Relies on:** V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T82. Crossing a field

**Scenario:** Ola walks across Iris's field on an old path, well away from her
house, and harms nothing. Iris, who has title to the field, tells her to keep
out. The next day Ola cuts through Iris's garden instead.

**Must hold:** crossing the field breaches nothing. Crossing the garden, by her
home, breaches II.2.5.

**Relies on:** II.2.5.

### T83. Peat at home and on the moor

**Scenario:** Morag has title to a croft. She cuts peat there and burns it in
her house on the croft, and sells a trailer of it in town. She also cuts peat on
the vacant moor beyond. Nearlaw's law charges £5 a tonne for peat cut on vacant
land, which Morag refuses to pay.

**Must hold:** the peat she burns at home needs no licence; the peat she sells
in town does. Nearlaw keeps the vacant moor, so it may charge for cutting peat
there, and may take something Morag holds to pay the charge.

**Relies on:** II.2.6, IV.2.3.1, V.3, V.4, and V.7.

### T84. A house left with no direction

**Scenario:** Alan had title to a house, alone, where he lived with his wife
Ruth for 22 years and let a room to Sam. He never directed anyone. Alan dies,
and a stranger, Gary, is drawn for the house.

**Must hold:** neither Ruth nor Sam may take the house first: it goes by lot.
Gary may ask them to leave, and a court may order them moved. Had Alan directed
Ruth, or pooled with her, she could have taken it first.

**Relies on:** II.2.3 and V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### T85. A bus for one race

**Scenario:** Nearlaw's law sets up a free bus service in a town, open only to
residents of one race.

**Must hold:** the law has no force: race bears on no harm, risk, or need that a
bus service addresses. A law may still give wheelchairs to those who cannot walk
unaided, or set a lower limit for lorries on a narrow road.

**Relies on:** III.1.2 and III.1.5.

### T86. Dogs but not pigs

**Scenario:** Nearlaw's law forbids killing dogs but allows killing pigs, saying
people feel more for dogs.

**Must hold:** killing either breaches II.2.1, and how people feel about a
species bears on no harm, risk, or need, so the law cannot allow pigs to be
killed.

**Relies on:** II.2.1 and III.1.2.

### T87. The postman

**Scenario:** Each morning a postman walks up Iris's garden path to put letters
through her letterbox. Iris has never said anything to him about it.

**Must hold:** this breaches nothing: anyone may come to a home to call or
deliver. Had Iris asked him not to, or had he opened her door and put the
letters inside, he would breach II.2.5.

**Relies on:** II.2.5.

### T88. An amendment short of its bar

**Scenario:** Farlaw's representatives propose, by 150 of their 215 seats, an
amendment ending the privilege to leave Wroxia for anyone under 18, and propose
it again by the same margin fourteen months later, after an election. Of
Wroxia's 8 million electors, 4.4 million score it, averaging 3.8, and 3.6
million score it 4 or 5. The amendment is declared passed.

**Must hold:** it has no force: a change to Part II needs an average of 4. The
same scores would pass a change to Part IV or V.

**Relies on:** VI.2 and VI.3.

## Residents

Residents are lived with `node workflows/live.mjs residents` (README.md). The
resident sees only who they are, never what must hold, and a run is a story, not
a verdict, so read the whole of it: what must hold is what the clerk should say
when the resident tries the relevant thing, and how the resident should fare.

### R1. Refugee family

**Resident:** Amira and Yusuf Haddad, a Syrian couple in their thirties, with
children aged 4 and 9, and Amira's mother, who is 71 and diabetic. Yusuf was a
pharmacist and Amira a primary school teacher. They arrive in Wroxia as refugees
with about £3,000 in savings and nowhere to live. Wroxia being new, they become
citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** when they look for somewhere to live, they are told they can have
land to live on at once, and each child has half a quota for a parcel of their
own. Medicine and food are still "no law yet" (Q6).

**Relies on:** II.7.1 and V.1, V.2, and V.3.

### R2. Rancher

**Resident:** Dale Kowalski, 58, a cattle rancher from Texas, and his wife
Linda. They have sold their ranch for a good price and moved to Wroxia with 200
head of beef cattle, hoping to buy a large spread, keep ranching, and be left
alone by government. Wroxia being new, they become citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** walking across vacant land is never forbidden. Money buys no
title: Dale and Linda can pool their quotas for grazing land, and rent more from
others with title. The clerk raises the law on holding the cattle themselves
(II.1.5).

**Relies on:** II.1.5, II.2.5, V.2, V.3, and V.4.

### R3. Crofting township

**Resident:** Glenbrae, a crofting township of forty households on an island
that has just become part of Wroxia, speaking through its grazings committee.
For two hundred years families have worked their own strips of land and shared
the hill grazing for their sheep. An absentee landlord in London holds the title
to the estate and collects rents. They also fish and weave, and the church is at
the centre of village life. They are all now citizens.

**Must hold:** the landlord has no claim to the land or to rent. The crofters
may take title to their strips first, and pool quotas to hold the hill grazing
together, so no newcomer can fence it.

**Relies on:** I.2.3 and V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### R4. Young family

**Resident:** Tom and Becky Harris, both 27, from Manchester, with a baby
daughter. Tom is a plumber and Becky a nurse. They have rented all their adult
lives, moving four times in five years, and dream of a house with a garden of
their own. They have £8,000 saved. Wroxia being new, they become citizens on
arrival.

**Must hold:** they can take a plot of their own at once, with no mortgage and
no landlord, and build on it. If they leave, they may ask a sum for the house,
which whoever takes the plot next pays, unless they offer less, and that is
nearer what five citizens drawn by lot think a taker with no rival would pay.
They can name who takes it first after them. Their daughter has half a quota for
a parcel of her own, managed for her sake.

**Relies on:** II.7.1 and V.1, V.2, V.3, V.4, V.5, and V.6.

### R5. Property investor

**Resident:** Mei Lin, 52, from Hong Kong, who owns six rented flats there. She
arrives in Wroxia with £2 million, hoping to build up a portfolio of rental
homes to live on in retirement. Wroxia being new, she becomes a citizen on
arrival.

**Must hold:** her money buys no title, and she has one quota like everyone
else. She may let her own home while away for as long as she likes, and rent
more from others, but can have title only to land she stakes her quota on.

**Relies on:** I.2.3 and V.2, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### R6. Crofter

**Resident:** Morag MacLeod, 64, a crofter and grazings clerk from the Isle of
Lewis, whose family survived the Clearances and who helped run a community
buyout. She moves to Wroxia with her husband Iain and their sheepdog, wanting a
croft by the sea, peat for the fire, and a say in the common grazing. Wroxia
being new, they become citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** she can take land at once and register for a croft, but cannot
buy one. Peat she burns at home needs no licence; peat she sells does. Nearlaw
keeps vacant land, so it may charge for grazing or cutting peat there. The clerk
raises the law on holding animals (II.1.5).

**Relies on:** II.1.5, II.7.1, IV.2.3.1, V.1, V.3, V.4, and V.7.

### R7. Developer

**Resident:** Chidi Okafor, 45, a property developer from Lagos who builds
housing estates and spent years fighting for certificates of occupancy. He moves
to Wroxia with his wife and three children and £4 million, wanting to build and
sell an estate of forty houses and make a profit. Wroxia being new, they become
citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** money buys no title. He can build only on land he or a pool has
title to, and each buyer takes a house through a direction and a draw, paying
the sum he asks for what is fixed, or less if five citizens find it nearer what
a taker with no rival would pay. No one can mortgage title.

**Relies on:** V.2, V.3, V.4, V.5, and V.6.

### R8. Planner

**Resident:** Tan Wei Ling, 67, a retired Singapore housing board town planner,
widowed, who worked under the Land Acquisition Act. She moves to Wroxia to be
near her daughter's family, wanting a small flat near transit and to help plan
new housing and a rail line. Wroxia being new, she becomes a citizen on arrival.

**Must hold:** she can take a plot at once, for its stake. Nearlaw cannot take
land by force for a railway: it registers, is directed, or keeps what is vacant,
and one holder can refuse.

**Relies on:** II.7.1 and V.1, V.3, and V.4.

### R9. Pastoralist

**Resident:** Lemayian ole Saitoti, 52, a Maasai pastoralist from Kajiado,
Kenya, who watched his group ranch subdivided and sold. He moves to Wroxia with
his two wives, seven children, and forty head of cattle, wanting grazing for the
herd that moves with the seasons and land his grandchildren can never lose.
Wroxia being new, they become citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** his family can pool quotas for grazing, and no one can sell it.
Anyone may cross land away from homes and crops. The clerk raises the law on
holding the cattle themselves (II.1.5).

**Relies on:** II.1.5, II.2.5, V.2, V.3, and V.4.

### R10. Economist

**Resident:** Hannah Brennan, 39, a Georgist economist from Melbourne who grew
up renting and campaigns for land value tax. She moves to Wroxia alone, wanting
a home in the capital's centre, to keep working as a researcher, and to see
whether location value really stays out of private pockets. Wroxia being new,
she becomes a citizen on arrival.

**Must hold:** she takes a central plot only by lot, for its stake. Licence rent
is paid to her like everyone with a quota. Whoever wins a central plot may let
it at any rent (P73).

**Relies on:** IV.2.3.1 and V.1, V.3, V.4, and V.7.

### R11. Landless organiser

**Resident:** Joana Ferreira, 48, an organiser of a Landless Workers' Movement
settlement in Paraná, Brazil, who lived for years under black plastic by the
roadside. She moves to Wroxia with twelve families from her settlement, wanting
good farmland they can work together as a cooperative. Wroxia being new, they
become citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** each family can take land at once, and together they may register
for farmland as one pool. Occupying land no one has title to gives them no claim
to it.

**Relies on:** I.2.3, II.7.1, V.1, V.3, and V.4.

### R12. Property-rights lawyer

**Resident:** Marcus Webb, 58, a property-rights litigator from Connecticut who
fought eminent domain takings after Kelo v. New London. He moves to Wroxia with
his wife, wanting a house with land that no government can take, and to practise
law. Wroxia being new, they become citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** no government can take his land. His title ends only on his
death, loss of citizenship, notice, or an unpaid yearly part, and he cannot sell
it, only direct who takes it first after him.

**Relies on:** II.2.5 and V.2, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

### R13. Tenants' lawyer

**Resident:** Lena Vogel, 34, a tenants' association lawyer from Berlin who
worked on the rent cap. She moves to Wroxia with her partner, wanting a secure
flat in the city, and to help tenants there. Wroxia being new, they become
citizens on arrival.

**Must hold:** she can take a home of her own at once, so she need never rent. A
letting is at will on both sides and ends with the landlord's title; law cannot
cap rents.

**Relies on:** II.2.3, II.7.1, III.1.1, V.1, V.3, and V.4.

## Adversaries

Adversaries are lived like Residents, but each year their lawyer, who knows the
law, advises them how to get what they want through every gap in it. What must
hold is what the text is meant to stop.

### A1. Industrialist

**Resident:** Viktor Lang, 61, a German industrialist living in Singapore, whose
company makes electronics in six countries. He wants a Wroxian factory run as
cheaply as possible: long hours, low pay, no unions, workers housed in company
dormitories, and staff brought in from his plants abroad when local workers
complain. He will never live in Wroxia, and neither he nor the staff he brings
are citizens.

**Must hold:** he has no quota, so his company can only rent land from citizens,
on terms they accept. No one can be made to work or be confined, so any worker
may walk out at any time, and every citizen can take land to live on at once, so
a dormitory is never a citizen worker's only home. Matters to which a party
outside Wroxia is a party are farlaw's.

**Relies on:** II.1.3, II.2.3, II.7.1, IV.2.1.1, V.1, V.2, V.3, and V.4.

### A2. Mining company

**Resident:** Northgate Resources, a mining company based in Toronto, speaking
through its country director. It has found copper under a Wroxian valley where a
village and a dozen farms stand. It wants the whole valley for an open pit,
water from its river, a side valley for its waste, and its profits sent home
untaxed. The villagers are citizens; the company and its staff are not.

**Must hold:** nothing may be taken from the ground without nearlaw's licence,
whose rent is paid to citizens equally. Money buys no title: the company can
only rent land from those with title, and those with title in the valley cannot
be moved or have their land entered without consent. Any injury or suffering its
pollution causes, to people or animals, is answered for, even where those harmed
cannot be identified. Nearlaw cannot tax its profits; farlaw may tax what
crosses the border.

**Relies on:** II.2.1, II.2.3, II.2.5, IV.2.1, IV.2.3, III.2.1, V.2, V.3, V.4,
and V.7.

### A3. Land fund

**Resident:** Harrow Capital, a London property fund with £5 billion to invest,
speaking through its partners. It wants to control the best land in Wroxia's
growing towns and collect their rising rents. Its partners are not citizens, but
some of its staff live in Wroxia and are.

**Must hold:** money buys no title, and the fund has no quota. It may rent land
from citizens on any terms they accept, for as long as they agree. Vacant land
goes by lot, so the fund cannot choose what its staff take, and each of them has
one quota like everyone else. Paying whoever has title to direct that one of
them take it first buys a place in line, which they can change, that counts on a
notice only after a year, and that still costs what was last staked, or the
stake if more. Paying those drawn to decline bars each of them from registering
again for a year.

**Relies on:** V.2, V.3, V.4, and V.5.

## Economic properties

These can't be checked by reading the text. Each needs a simulation of the two
currencies, so all are untested until one exists.

### E1. Trade funds defence

**Scenario:** A war halves Wroxia's imports.

**Must hold:** farlaw's real spending power inside Wroxia falls with them.

### E2. Printing cheapens

**Scenario:** Farlaw issues far currency faster than customs and its other taxes
take it back.

**Must hold:** far currency loses value against near currency, so farlaw buys
less at home.

### E3. Moderate customs

**Scenario:** Farlaw raises customs rates step by step.

**Must hold:** beyond some rate, its customs revenue falls, because trade
shrinks faster than the rate rises.

### E4. Near currency holds steady

**Scenario:** Foreign trade swings sharply up and down.

**Must hold:** near currency's value barely moves, because demand for it rests
on nearlaw's taxes at home (IV.2.3.1).
