# Constitution of Wroxia

## Preamble

We who live in Wroxia establish this constitution so that every being able to fare well or badly is safe from harm, so that each person is as free as that safety allows, and so that the law stays short enough for everyone it governs to know it.

## Part I: Terms and interpretation

### Definitions

1. **Sentient** (adjective): having subjective experience, so that wellbeing or suffering can be felt.
2. **Patient** (noun): any sentient being.
3. **Agent** (noun): a patient able to understand others’ rights and privileges and to act on that understanding.
4. **Citizen** (noun): a patient recognised as a citizen by law. Demonym: Wroxian.
5. **Right** (noun): an entitlement that no law, condition or waiver can remove.
6. **Privilege** (noun): an entitlement that holds unless one of its stated conditions is met, and that its holder may waive.
7. **Universal** (adjective, of a right or privilege): held by every patient.
8. **National** (adjective, of a right or privilege): held by every citizen.
9. **Negative** (adjective, of a right or privilege): fulfilled by others refraining from action.
10. **Positive** (adjective, of a right or privilege): fulfilled by a government acting.
11. **Occupancy** (noun): making land or a building one’s home or workplace.
12. **Holding** (noun): keeping a thing for one’s own use.
13. **Suffering** (noun): pain, fear or distress felt by a patient.
14. **Torture** (noun): deliberately causing severe suffering, to punish, to coerce, or for its own sake.
15. **Duress** (noun): pressure that leaves no reasonable choice.
16. **Court** (noun): a body independent of the parties that hears and decides a case under law.

### Application

1. Every animal with a central nervous system is presumed sentient.
2. Only an agent can breach a right or privilege. A human is presumed to have been an agent when acting, unless evidence shows otherwise. Capacity is established under Instrument 3.
3. Land and buildings are occupied by those who live or work there, and no one has any claim to them except by occupancy.
4. A thing one has lent is still held. Holding ends only when the thing is given away or discarded, and no one has any claim to a thing except by holding it.
5. A waiver, consent, direction or renunciation given under duress, or obtained by deception, is void.

## Part II: Universal

### Article 1. Universal negative rights

Every patient has the right:

1. To freedom from torture.
2. Not to be compelled to reveal their thoughts, their private communications, or any key or password.
3. Not to be compelled to work, or to take part in any public scheme of work or exchange.
4. Not to have their body, after death, held or used except as they directed, or under Instrument 2 (_default purpose_ safe care and disposal, _Advocate_ Negative Rights Advocate).
5. Not to be held by anyone as a thing.

### Article 2. Universal negative privileges

Every patient has the privilege:

1. Not to be killed or injured, nor made to suffer, by others. It may be waived as to death only through the Negative Privileges Advocate. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
   - An agent who would otherwise die for persistent lack of food, with no other practicable food, may kill to eat.
2. Not to be put at unreasonable risk of death, injury or suffering by others. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
3. Not to be confined, or moved from where they live. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
4. To leave Wroxia.
5. Not to have land or buildings they occupy entered or used without their consent. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
6. Not to have things they hold taken, damaged or used without their consent. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
   - A thing may be taken to make restitution owed under Article 8.
7. Not to be watched or recorded inside a place they occupy, except by those they have let in; nor to have communications or records about them read or copied by anyone they did not address or entrust them to, nor shared by anyone, without their consent. Conditions:
   - They may be shared to bring or decide a claim of breach.
8. If unable to consent, to have nothing done to them except for their own sake. Conditions:
   - Defence under 2.9.
9. Where a privilege permits defence, it may be limited to defend a patient from imminent death, injury or suffering, only so far and for so long as necessary, without causing harm out of proportion to what is prevented. Expression protected by Article 7.3 is no ground for such a limit.

### Article 3. Universal positive rights

Every patient has the right:

1. To know the current date and time.
2. To receive, on request, a written statement of whether they are a citizen.

## Part III: National

### Article 4. Citizenship

1. Citizenship is gained and lost as law provides.
2. Any patient may apply for citizenship, or have someone apply on its behalf.
3. Any citizen may renounce their citizenship.

### Article 5. National negative rights

Every citizen has the right:

1. To enter Wroxia while unladen.

### Article 6. National positive privileges

Every citizen has the privilege:

1. Of equitable public free speech and broadcast.

## Part IV: Liberty and law

### Article 7. Liberty

1. No law may forbid an act that breaches no right or privilege.
2. A law may distinguish between patients by a trait only if it would make the same distinction between two humans who differed in that trait.
3. Expression alone never breaches a right or privilege, except: a threat of harm to a patient; a demand backed by duress; communications kept up towards someone who has asked for them to stop; or sharing what Article 2.7 protects.
4. Within those limits, law may build freely on this constitution: founding institutions, raising taxes and providing services.
5. A law contrary to this constitution has no force.

### Article 8. Answering for harm

1. An agent who breaches a right or privilege shall make restitution to those harmed, as far as it can be made.
2. Restitution may be made in kind: restoring the harmed to safety, health and a place to live, as far as can be done, at the cost of whoever caused the harm.
3. Restitution owed is paid to those harmed at once from public funds, and the government that paid recovers it from whoever caused the harm.
4. Each government answers as an agent for everything done in its name.
5. Any act done in a government’s name that limits a privilege stands as a breach, unless that government shows that one of the privilege’s conditions was met.
6. Anyone acting in a government’s name who accepts anything of value from anyone but that government, in exchange for how they act in that role, loses the role, and whatever they did in exchange is void. Offering such a thing breaches nothing.
7. Anyone harmed, or an Advocate on behalf of any patient, may bring a claim of breach.
8. A disputed claim of breach is decided by a court, after each party has had a chance to answer the claim and evidence. The court gives reasons; each party may have the claim reviewed once by another court.
9. Anyone confined or moved from where they live without their consent may require prompt review by a court, where whoever imposed it bears the burden of justifying it.

## Part V: Government

### Article 9. Nearlaw and Farlaw

1. Wroxia has two governments:
   1. Farlaw governs every matter to which a power or party outside Wroxia is a party, and anything while it crosses Wroxia’s border.
   2. Nearlaw governs every other matter.
2. Each government issues its own currency.
3. Each government raises taxes only from matters within its own domain, payable only in its own currency.
4. Each government may keep its own forces and police, which enforce only that government’s law.
5. Every matter is nearlaw unless shown to be farlaw. Which government a matter belongs to may be challenged only by someone affected by it, an Advocate, or either government, and is then decided by a boundary court, which rules on nothing else.
6. Nearlaw’s laws are made by a body chosen from volunteering citizens by Instrument 1 (_maximum service_ 25 years, _round interval_ one year, _reconfirmed fraction_ one third). It decides under 9.8.
7. Farlaw’s laws are made by representatives elected by citizens from among citizens who volunteer to stand. A separate body chosen from volunteering citizens by Instrument 1 (_maximum service_ 25 years, _round interval_ one year, _reconfirmed fraction_ one third) may veto a proposed law before it takes effect. Both bodies decide under 9.8.
8. A body deciding under this clause decides by a majority of those voting, with more than half its seats taking part.

### Article 10. Rendering

1. Positive rights and privileges are owed by the governments, each within its own domain.
2. Each government renders them through people it hires or deputises.

### Article 11. Advocates

1. There are four Advocates: for negative rights, positive rights, negative privileges and positive privileges.
2. Each Advocate defends its kind against breach by anyone, including either government.
3. Where a patient cannot act for itself, the Negative Privileges Advocate acts for it, and receives any restitution on its behalf, to be used only for its own sake.
4. Each Advocate is chosen by lot from among citizens, for a fixed term.
