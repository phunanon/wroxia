# Precedents

16 November 2026, by Claude Fable. For each belief: what the world has tried
that resembles it, how it went, how ordinary people took to it, and the question
it leaves for Wroxia. Everything is from memory, not from sources: treat dates
and figures as leads to check, not facts to quote.

## A short constitution

The United States' is about 7,600 words with its amendments; India's is about
145,000. Shortness did not keep American law short: the meaning moved into tens
of thousands of pages of court rulings, and power moved to whoever interprets.
**For Wroxia:** a short text makes the interpreter the real legislator. Who the
courts are (P46), and whether one court's reading binds the next, matters more
here than in a long constitution.

## Sentient life

- **Constitutional mentions.** Switzerland ("the dignity of the creature",
  1992), Germany (Article 20a, 2002), and India (a citizen's duty of compassion)
  all name animals. As state aims without anyone to enforce them, they have lost
  to farming every time.
- **Sentience lists.** The UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 covers
  vertebrates, cephalopods, and decapods: I.2.1's list, less the insects.
- **An advocate.** The canton of Zurich had a public animal lawyer from 1992 to
  2010, and prosecutions rose sharply. A 2010 referendum to give every canton
  one lost heavily.
- **Personhood cases.** An Argentine court called the orangutan Sandra a
  "non-human person" (2015); New York's highest court refused the same for the
  elephant Happy (2022).

No state has gone as far as Part II. **Reception:** this is where most people
will turn up their noses, at meat and at pest control, and the Zurich vote shows
it even among the sympathetic. **For Wroxia:** the Advocates are the piece every
precedent lacked. What the precedents cannot tell you is how a whole population
lives under it, which is what Residents are for.

## An equal share of land

- **The Russian mir** redivided village land by household size every few years.
  It kept everyone fed and no one landless, but nobody improved a strip they
  might lose, and the Stolypin reforms (1906) dissolved it.
- **The Mexican ejido** (1917 to 1992) gave villagers land they could use but
  never sell, let, or mortgage. It prevented dispossession for generations. But
  holders who left let their plots anyway, illegally, and nobody could borrow to
  build.
- **China's household responsibility system** (from about 1980) shares village
  land per head. It ended famine fast. Where villages reallocated often, farmers
  invested less.
- **Singapore** ballots new public flats, on 99-year leases, to citizens, who
  must live in them for five years before selling or letting. About four in five
  people live in one, and it is popular.
- **Georgia's land lotteries** (1805 to 1833) gave land by lot to citizens; many
  winners sold at once to speculators.
- **The US Homestead Act** (1862) required five years' residence; people built
  sham cabins and hired stand-ins.

**For Wroxia:** three lessons recur. Paying the leaver for improvements is not
optional (the mir, the ejido): V.6 is right to exist. Where land cannot be sold,
a hidden letting market grows (the ejido): P73. And any residence test invites
pretence (homesteads), though Singapore shows one can work where records are
good. Non-transferable quotas already avoid Georgia's fate.

## Paying for the state: land, heads, and hours

- **Land value taxes** exist in Denmark, Estonia, Taiwan, and some Pennsylvania
  towns. Economists of every school call them the least distorting tax. They
  have never raised most of a state's revenue, mostly because landowners vote.
- **Frozen rates.** California's Proposition 13 (1978) fixed each home's tax
  near its purchase price until sold. Neighbours pay wildly different sums, and
  people do not move. V.8.1's rate that "never rises while they hold it" is the
  same design.
- **Poll taxes in money.** England's of 1380 set off the Peasants' Revolt.
  Thatcher's Community Charge (1990) brought riots and mass non-payment, and was
  gone by 1993. Enforcing it needed a register of persons, so people dropped off
  the electoral roll to hide.
- **Duties in hours.** French villagers owed three days a year on local roads
  from 1836 into the twentieth century; the rich paid cash instead, and it was
  resented for that. Rwanda's umuganda takes one morning a month from every
  adult, with a fine for absence. Jury service is the hours duty the
  English-speaking world accepts almost without complaint.

**For Wroxia:** hours duties are tolerated when they are local, visibly useful,
and nobody can buy their way out. V.9 has the third; "work it arranges" does not
yet promise the first two. Tying the duty to title, not to persons, avoids the
register that sank the Community Charge.

## Lot instead of election

- **Athens** filled its council of 500 and most offices by lot, for one year,
  twice in a lifetime at most.
- **Ireland's Citizens' Assembly** (99 people, from 2016) proposed the abortion
  referendum that passed two to one in 2018. **East Belgium** has had a standing
  council by lot since 2019. **France's Climate Convention** (150 people, 2019)
  saw its proposals watered down, to the members' public anger.

No modern state lets a body chosen by lot make binding law. The modern ones are
popular and trusted more than parliaments. **For Wroxia:** Instrument 1 departs
from every precedent in two ways. Terms: all of them use short single terms, to
stop members becoming professionals worth lobbying, where sticky sortition
allows ~~25~~ 24 years. Volunteers: modern assemblies invite at random and then
balance by age, sex, and region, because those who "put themselves forward" are
older, richer, and more male. Both departures may be deliberate; both deserve a
Debate.

VI.3's rule that two fifths of all electors must approve is better than the
usual turnout rule: Italy requires half to vote, so opponents win by staying
home.

## Drugs

Portugal (2001) stopped prosecuting possession and sent users to health panels;
deaths and HIV fell, and it held up while treatment was funded. Oregon (2020)
decriminalised without building treatment, and reversed it in 2024. No country
permits open sale of everything. **For Wroxia:** the health half is what makes
the liberty half publicly bearable, which strengthens Q6.

## Restitution, not punishment

- **New Zealand** pays everyone injured by accident from a public fund (ACC,
  1974), fault or not, and abolished the right to sue. It is cheap and
  well-liked. III.2.3 is close kin, and adds recovery from whoever caused it.
- **New Zealand's family group conferences** (1989), from Māori practice, and
  **Rwanda's gacaca** courts are the best-known restorative processes.
- **Wergild and blood money.** Early English and many other laws knew only
  payment for harm. The standing complaint was that the rich could afford to
  hurt people.

**For Wroxia:** restitution alone turns harm into a price. The pressing cases
are the one who can pay and keeps doing it, and the one who cannot pay and keeps
doing it (P11).

## No enforceable promises

Almost unknown as a rule. Where courts would not or could not enforce, trade ran
on reputation inside tight groups: medieval Maghribi merchants, diamond dealers,
and gamblers' "debts of honour". It works among people who will meet again, and
it keeps strangers out. **For Wroxia:** expect cash on delivery, small firms,
and communities that vouch for their members; five of eight residents hurt by
broken promises (P51) is what the precedents predict.

## Family unknown; children and guardians

No state ignores families entirely. Israel's kibbutzim raised children in
communal houses, and gave it up by the 1990s because parents wanted them home.
But much of modern family law has already moved from blood to function, and that
is usable:

- **Parental responsibility** (England, Children Act 1989) belongs to whoever is
  raising the child, and can be held by people who are not the parents.
- **Growing capacity.** The Gillick ruling (1985) lets a child decide a matter
  once they understand it, whatever their age. The UN Convention on the Rights
  of the Child speaks of "evolving capacities".
- **Nominated helpers.** British Columbia (1996) lets any adult name someone to
  decide with or for them, with no court involved. Sweden abolished full
  guardianship of adults in 1989 in favour of a mentor who needs the person's
  consent.
- **The state as last resort.** Every system has it, and children raised by the
  state do worse almost everywhere.

**For Wroxia:** this is P47 already working in real law. A constitution can stay
blind to families and still say: whoever a child lives with acts for them; the
child's own say grows with their understanding (I.1.3's definition of an agent
already does this); anyone may name who acts for them; the Advocate comes last
(Q11).

## Roaming and access

Sweden, Norway, and Scotland (2003) all allow walking and resting on private
land, away from homes and crops. They are loved, and the quarrels are small:
litter, dogs among sheep, and camping. The postman's implied licence, withdrawn
by a word, is long-settled English law. II.2.5 matches both.

## Extraction

Alaska has paid every resident an equal yearly share of oil income since 1982.
It is the most popular thing the state does, and no politician dares touch it.
**For Wroxia:** V.7's equal payment will be loved. The Alaskan warning is that
people paid by extraction vote for more of it, and V.7 limits nothing yet (P86).

## Cash and decaying money

The Austrian town of Wörgl (1932) issued notes that lost one per cent a month
unless a stamp was bought and stuck on. They changed hands fast, the town built
roads and a bridge in a depression, and the central bank shut it down after a
year. **For Wroxia:** a stamp keeps a note anonymous, where IV.2.3.1's exchange
of notes need not (Q12). People also ran from decaying notes into other stores
of value; in Wroxia that means haven, which matters for how the two currencies
meet.
