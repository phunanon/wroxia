# The Beliefs against the text

16 November 2026, by Claude Fable, reading the Constitution and Instruments as
committed at `daeca7d` against Beliefs.md. It says, belief by belief, whether
the text delivers it, and where two beliefs pull apart. It is a reading, not a
Hearing: nothing here was judged by a fresh model. Only the Problems cited were
read.

## Delivered

- **Words.** The Constitution and Instruments are about 3,800 words.
- **Veganism.** Every right in II.1 and II.2 belongs to patients, not humans;
  I.2.1 presumes most animals sentient; "as far as possible and practicable"
  became "harm that could not practicably be avoided" (II.2.1). II.1.5 ends
  owning animals outright. Q7 is the open edge.
- **Land distribution.** One equal quota each (II.7.1, V.2), never transferable.
  Note that it is the quota that is equal, not the land: wanted land costs more
  quota (V.3.3).
- **Enfranchisement.** Electors are 16 and over (I.1.17), and all of them score
  members (Instrument 1) and amendments (VI.3).
- **Justice.** III.2 knows only restitution; there is no punishment anywhere.
  The three Observations are each answered: public funds pay first and the
  government disputes as a party (inflated harm); admitting harm costs only the
  restitution (amends); and an Advocate may claim for anyone (III.2.8,
  coercion).
- **Privacy.** II.1.2 and II.2.7, with government speech excluded.
- **Drugs.** III.1.1 forbids forbidding them. Nothing treats addiction as a
  health issue, because nothing yet provides health care at all (Q6).
- **Barter.** IV.2.3.1 lists everything Nearlaw may tax, and exchange is not on
  it. Farlaw's taxes are unlisted (P32), so barter across the border is not yet
  safe.
- **Tenancy.** Unlegislated, and kept rare by two things: everyone has land at
  once (V.3.4), and consent stands only until withdrawn (I.2.5), so no lease
  binds.
- **Roaming** and **Implied right of access.** The two conditions of II.2.5,
  almost word for word.
- **Extraction.** Licensed (V.7); the limiting is still to do (P86).
- **Separated domains.** Two governments, two currencies, two forces, four
  Advocates. Courts are the missing domain (P46).

## Partly delivered

- **Public land.** A government "takes land like anyone, staking nothing"
  (V.3.1), so only by draw or direction. But the draw gives "one chance for each
  quota they register" (V.3.2), and a government registers none: read plainly,
  it can never win a contested draw. Worth a case.
- **Money.** Land is reached by quota, never money. But contested land carries
  the land charge (V.8), payable only in hearth (IV.2.3), so keeping wanted land
  needs money. Service (V.9) is the only charge that honours this belief.
- **Democracy.** Nearlaw is drawn by lot; Farlaw is still elected
  representatives (IV.3.2), checked by a veto body drawn by lot. Whether that
  remnant of representative democracy is wanted is for P36 and P67. Against the
  tyranny of the majority stand the absolute rights, III.1.1, and VI.3's
  thresholds.
- **Peace.** Restitution without punishment, and III.2.5 puts the burden on
  government. But nothing yet stops harm that is continuing (P11, P48).
- **Private domination.** The quota caps land, and duress voids consent. Nothing
  caps money or things (P39).
- **Distortion.** The land charge falls on contest, which is close to the least
  distorting tax known. But a rate that "never rises while they hold it" (V.8.1)
  rewards staying put, and the cliff between a starter plot and any other title
  (P87) rewards staying small.

## Not yet delivered

- **Capitalism.** Nothing makes title depend on occupancy. One may hold title,
  live elsewhere, and let (P73). The quota keeps each absentee small, but does
  not disallow them. Q10.
- **Family.** The state does not know families, but in their absence it knows
  only itself: a child's quota is "managed for their sake" (V.2.1) by no one
  named, and whoever cannot act for themselves falls to an Advocate (IV.4.3, now
  yielding to IV.5, which was I.2.6 until 17 November). As written, the state is
  every child's default guardian. Q11.
- **Public remuneration.** Service is counted in hours (V.9), but nothing says
  how governments account or pay (P27).
- **Cash.** IV.2.3.1 mentions notes in passing. Nothing guarantees that notes
  exist, or that the yearly exchange is anonymous. Q12.

## Where beliefs pull apart

1. **Taxation is essential; no one may be compelled to work or to join a scheme
   of exchange (II.1.3); money is optional.** Together these leave only one kind
   of tax: something given up for a benefit one could decline. V.8 and V.9 both
   have that shape, with title as the benefit. A true poll tax, in hours or
   money, is impossible while starter plots exist and II.1.3 is absolute, and
   I.1.5 puts II.1.3 beyond amendment. V.9 is as near to the hours poll tax of
   the last Observation as this constitution can come.
2. **Land held is a poor measure of reliance on the state; yet the land charge
   stands.** The two need not conflict, because the charge can be justified
   differently: not as payment for the state, but as payment to everyone else
   for keeping them off land they wanted. The text already treats extraction
   rent and surplus from public land this way, paying both equally to everyone
   with a quota. Q9.
3. **Family is unknown; children must be safe.** See Q11.
4. **Cash preserves privacy; hearth is charged yearly on every holding.** The
   charge works by recalling notes, and recall is where anonymity is lost. Q12.
5. **No absentee ownership; privacy.** Any rule that title needs occupancy needs
   someone to check who lives where. Q10 suggests a rule that needs no
   inspector.
