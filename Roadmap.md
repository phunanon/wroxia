# Roadmap

What a constitution typically covers, which of it Wroxia needs, and how far the
text has come in each. The areas follow the topics the Comparative Constitutions
Project finds in most national constitutions, plus private law, which Wroxia
needs because its law may forbid only what breaches an entitlement (III.1.1).

Each area is done when the text answers every question in its row. **Status:**
_covered_ means it does; _partial_ means it answers some; _missing_ means none;
_out_ means deliberately left to ordinary law or a later version.

| Area                        | Done when the text says                                                       | Where              | Status  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------- |
| Purpose                     | What the constitution is for                                                  | Preamble           | covered |
| Terms                       | What its key words mean, and how it is read                                   | Part I             | covered |
| Supremacy                   | What law may do, and what happens to law that breaks it                       | III.1              | covered |
| Protection from harm        | What no one may do to anyone, and when that may be limited                    | II.1, II.2         | covered |
| Remedies                    | What is owed for a breach, who pays, and how it is recovered                  | III.2              | partial |
| Positive entitlements       | What each person is owed: shelter, food, health, schooling, or none           | II.3, II.4, II.7–8 | partial |
| Land and property           | Who may hold land and things, how they are gained, passed on, and ended       | I.2, Part V        | covered |
| Promises and organisations  | Whether agreements bind, and whether groups can hold, owe, or act             | none               | missing |
| Citizenship                 | Who is a citizen, how it is gained and lost, and what non-citizens may do     | IV.1               | partial |
| Division of government      | Which government does what, and who settles disputes between them             | IV.2               | covered |
| Law-making                  | Who makes each government's law, how, and what checks them                    | IV.3, Instr. 1, 4  | partial |
| Elections                   | How representatives are chosen, for how long, and by what system              | Instrument 5       | missing |
| Leadership                  | Who runs each government day to day, how they are chosen, and how removed     | none               | missing |
| Courts                      | Who judges, how they are chosen and kept independent, and what they may order | I.1.14, III.2.9–10 | partial |
| Ombudsmen                   | Who defends entitlements against governments, and with what powers            | IV.4               | partial |
| Public finance              | What each government may tax, how money is issued, and how each is funded     | IV.2.2–3           | partial |
| Defence and foreign affairs | Who may use force abroad, make treaties, and act in emergencies, within what  | IV.2.4, IV.2.6     | partial |
| Amendment                   | How each part of the text can change, and how hard that is                    | Part VI            | covered |
| Changeover                  | When the text takes effect, and what happens to land, debts, and office then  | V.5                | partial |
| Local government            | Whether places or communities govern themselves                               | none               | out     |
| Language and symbols        | Official languages, flag, and the like                                        | none               | out     |

## Done when

Version 1 is done when every area not marked _out_ is _covered_, and the
Constitution and Instruments together stay under 10,000 words, as
[Beliefs](Beliefs.md) asks (3,437 on 12 November 2026).

## Suggested order

These make everything already written enforceable, so they come first:

1. **Courts**: who judges, how they are chosen, and what they may order.
2. **Elections**, then **leadership**.
3. **Changeover**: what happens on the day land joins Wroxia.

Then public finance, promises and organisations, citizenship, and positive
entitlements.
